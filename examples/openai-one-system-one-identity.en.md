## OpenAI · "one system, one identity" holds in the visual layer and blurs in the naming layer

**Where the message travels**

Rebranding brief → visual identity unified → **naming and entry points were never in scope** → the system a user meets splits again

| Connection | What is actually there |
|---|---|
| Brief → visual system | Six or seven scattered typefaces became one, OpenAI Sans, and the logo and palette reached every product |
| Visual system → product entry points | openai.com and chatgpt.com are separate, and the front page introduces Chat, Work, and Codex as three ways in |
| Entry points → names to learn | The model picker still carries Instant, Thinking, Pro, and a legacy tab |

**What the company says**

At the February 2025 rebrand, OpenAI head of design Veit Moeller described the instruction he was given: "Sam asked us to create one system, one identity." In the same interview he said that in the three years since ChatGPT launched, nobody had had time to work on a unified identity system. [Fast Company, February 2025](https://www.fastcompany.com/91273217/open-ai-rebrand-chat-gpt-logo)

The problem the brief aimed at is on the record too. The visual identity was inconsistent enough that it often felt like it represented several teams rather than one company. [Creative Review, 6 February 2025](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/openai-brand-refresh/)

**What the product shows**

In the visual layer the promise held. OpenAI Sans replaced the scattered typefaces, and the refined blossom logo, the "point" that stands for the cursor, and a new palette and grid reached openai.com, ChatGPT on web and in apps, and the research papers. Open openai.com and chatgpt.com side by side today and the same identity is there.

The naming layer is different. As of August 2026 the ChatGPT front page introduces not one product but three ways in: Chat, Work, and Codex. Opening `openai.com/chatgpt/overview` lands you on `chatgpt.com`. One brand, two domains. [ChatGPT Overview](https://chatgpt.com/overview)

The model names remain as well. OpenAI's own release notes tell users to "check the model picker" in the product they use, and note that a given model "will not appear as a selectable model in the model picker" or is "available to paid users only via model settings." The first half of 2026 alone introduced GPT-5.3 Instant, 5.4 Thinking, 5.4 Pro, 5.4 mini, 5.5 Instant, and 5.6 Sol. [Model release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9624314-model-release-notes)

**Where it changes**

The symptom the brief set out to remove was a company that looked like several teams. In type, color, and marks, that symptom is gone.

But the layer a user actually runs into is not that one. You have to know where to go in - Chat or Work or Codex - what to pick once you are there - Instant or Thinking or Pro - and when the thing you were using disappears. This naming layer sat outside the brief, and the symptom the brief targeted still lives there.

Less a broken promise than a mismatch of layers: the layer the wording governed is not the layer where a reader feels whether this is one system. The identity was unified. The system was not.

**Why it may be this way**

Two explanations both fit what can be observed.

One is scope. This sentence was an instruction to a design team, not a claim made to customers, and its subject was the visual identity from the start. On that reading the crowded naming layer is not a failure but an area the brief never covered.

The other is speed. OpenAI acknowledged that its growth had outpaced the evolution of its design system, and that pace continued after the rebrand. Models and products kept arriving through the following year and a half, and the naming scheme did not keep up.

Separating the two would take an internal naming principle or the scoping document for the rebrand. Public material does not settle it.
