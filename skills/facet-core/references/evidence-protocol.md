# Evidence Protocol

## Source order

Prefer sources according to the claim being tested:

1. Current official product, policy, legal, technical, support, and pricing materials
2. Regulators, standards bodies, court records, audited disclosures, and government publications
3. Identifiable customer evidence and high-quality independent reporting or research
4. Reviews and community reports for experienced behavior, with scope and bias checks
5. First-party campaigns, testimonials, and case studies, labeled as first-party

Do not let source prestige substitute for directness. A regulator may establish a legal event without measuring product experience; a product page may establish a feature without proving its outcome.

For the promise itself, prefer a current homepage, About or mission page, product proposition, investor material, or brand manifesto where the company intentionally foregrounds the wording. A sentence repeated incidentally in legal boilerplate, privacy policies, footers, archived campaigns, or third-party summaries does not by itself establish the company's current central promise.

## Evidence record

For each material item, retain:

- exact claim or observation
- supported claim dimensions
- surface
- direct source URL
- publication or effective date
- first-party or independent status
- product, market, audience, and time scope
- what the source does not establish

## Observation and inference

Write observations before causes.

- Observation: `The default flow introduces three additional steps before the stated safety control can be enabled.`
- Inference: `Safety may be implemented as an optional layer rather than a default constraint.`

Do not convert correlation, chronology, organizational structure, or incentives into a cause without a competing-hypothesis test.

## Scope checks

- Product evidence does not automatically describe the company.
- One market does not establish global behavior.
- One campaign does not establish the brand's durable promise.
- One review does not establish a repeated experience pattern.
- A policy commitment does not establish implementation or effectiveness.
- A feature does not establish priority when trade-offs are unobserved.

## Missing and conflicting evidence

Use:

- `Omitted` when a relevant claim is not found where it would reasonably be expected and no contrary evidence exists.
- `Unknown` when evidence is unavailable, inaccessible, too indirect, or materially conflicting.
- `Contradicted` only when direct evidence is incompatible with the claim on the same dimensions and scope.

Public silence may reflect capability absence, disclosure policy, legal constraints, poor discoverability, or incomplete research. Do not choose among these explanations without evidence.

## Reviews

Use reviews to test experienced implementation, communication, friction, support, continuity, and outcome reports. Check date, product, branch, market, duplication, incentives, and provider specificity. Do not turn ratings into a universal quality score or use anecdotes as population-level proof.
