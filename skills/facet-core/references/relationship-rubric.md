# Relationship Rubric

Assign labels only after claim/evidence verification and only between comparable claim dimensions.

## Reinforced

The material meaning is preserved and the downstream surface adds implementation, specificity, proof, or experienced confirmation.

## Preserved

Expression changes, but the material truth conditions and decision-relevant expectation remain substantially the same.

## Compressed

Detail is omitted or simplified, but the omission would not materially change the user's expectation of the promise.

## Divergent

The promise remains recognizable, but one or more dimensions change enough to create a materially different real-world expectation.

Apply the materiality test:

> If the omitted or changed fact were known from the start, would it materially change the expectation created by the promise?

- No: `Compressed`
- Yes: `Divergent`

## Omitted

A relevant claim or implementation is not found on a surface where it would reasonably be expected, with no contrary evidence present. Omission is not contradiction.

## Contradicted

Direct evidence is materially incompatible with the claim on the same dimensions and scope. Use sparingly; a narrower implementation is usually `Divergent` rather than `Contradicted`.

## Unknown

The evidence cannot support a defensible relationship judgment. Prefer `Unknown` to speculation.

## Critical dimensions by claim type

| Claim type | Inspect first |
|---|---|
| Speed or ease | Time, effort, condition |
| Outcome | Outcome, scope, condition |
| Access | Scope, cost, geography, eligibility |
| Safety or priority | Default, friction, architecture, trade-off, effectiveness |

A safety feature proves capability or investment. It does not by itself prove that safety governs the architecture when safety conflicts with engagement, growth, speed, or cost.
